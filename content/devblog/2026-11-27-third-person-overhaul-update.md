Today’s work was all about feel. OPEN//77’s third-person mode just received a major upgrade, with a full pass on camera behavior, movement presentation, and gameplay transitions. For a Cyberpunk 2077 multiplayer mod, this kind of improvement matters a lot: players spend every second looking through that camera, reading motion, and reacting to combat, so small gains stack up into a much better experience.

## A much stronger third-person camera

The headline change is a big overhaul to third-person camera framing. The new behavior aims for a more modern action-game feel, with a smoother free-orbit setup and better on-screen composition while moving around Night City.

In practical terms, the camera now does a better job keeping the player readable without feeling stiff or glued in place. Movement should look more natural, the camera should feel less awkward during fast direction changes, and the overall presentation is closer to what players expect from polished third-person action games.

The team also improved stabilization and contextual framing. That means the camera can react more cleanly depending on what the player is doing, instead of treating every moment the same way. Quiet movement, combat pressure, and situational actions can now feel more intentionally framed instead of improvised.

## Native 60 FPS locomotion changes the feel immediately

One of the most important upgrades in this pass is native 60 FPS locomotion in third-person. That is the kind of change players feel right away, even before they can explain why.

Movement animation, direction changes, and body alignment all benefit when locomotion updates at this level. Sprinting, turning, stopping, and re-accelerating should feel more responsive and much less muddy. In a Cyberpunk 2077 co-op or Cyberpunk 2077 online setting, that readability matters not just for your own character, but for how other players look and react around you.

This work also included improvements to locomotion handling and character alignment, helping movement look less robotic and more grounded during regular gameplay.

## Better cover, combat, and vehicle transitions

This pass was not limited to basic running around. The team also polished several situations that usually expose rough edges in third-person systems.

Cover corner peeking received extra attention, which should help third-person gameplay feel more deliberate when using walls and corners. Combat-related camera behavior also saw improvements, giving fights a more controlled and readable presentation.

Vehicle transitions were another focus area. Moving between on-foot gameplay and driving can easily feel clunky if the camera or character presentation is even slightly off. This update adds polish to those transitions, helping the experience stay smoother when jumping in and out of vehicles.

Drive-by presentation also got love, which is especially important for a Cyberpunk 2077 dedicated server experience where players will want vehicles to be more than just transportation.

## A small fix with a big quality-of-life impact

Alongside the major overhaul, the team fixed the Alt camera lock behavior in third-person.

This is the kind of issue that sounds minor on paper, but can be surprisingly noticeable in real play. Camera controls are something players touch constantly, so even small fixes can make the game feel dramatically more reliable and less distracting.

## Why this matters for OPEN//77

A good multiplayer game is not only about netcode, features, or server systems. It is also about whether moving through the world feels good minute to minute. That is especially true for a Cyberpunk 2077 multiplayer project, where players want style, motion, and cinematic energy alongside solid mechanics.

This update moves OPEN//77 closer to that goal. The camera is smarter, locomotion is smoother, and common gameplay states like cover, combat, and vehicles now have more polish. None of this is flashy in the same way as a brand-new game mode, but it is exactly the kind of work that makes Night City online feel more playable, more immersive, and more worth coming back to.

For players waiting on OPEN//77, this is a strong sign that the team is not only building systems, but also shaping the moment-to-moment experience that will define CP2077 multiplayer when people finally jump in.
