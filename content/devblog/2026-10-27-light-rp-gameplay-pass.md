Today’s work was all about features players and server owners will actually feel. **Light RP** received a major gameplay pass with upgrades to inventory, medical gameplay, vehicle customization, and moderation tools, while the base multiplayer build also got a cleaner default starting point for the female character seed.

For anyone following OPEN//77 as a **Cyberpunk 2077 multiplayer mod**, this was the kind of update that makes the world feel closer to a real playable loop rather than just a technical foundation.

## Light RP inventory feels more usable in motion

A big part of roleplay and co-op flow is how quickly players can read their gear, swap items, and react under pressure. The inventory and equipment layout in Light RP was redesigned to make status and equipped items clearer at a glance.

Quick access also improved. The first row of quick slots is now bound, and the held-Tab hotbar was integrated as part of the same flow. That means fewer menu fights and faster item use during combat, driving, or emergency situations.

Grenades also got an important behavior fix. Instead of feeling like awkward stacked gadgets, they now behave as finite consumables with proper charge reconciliation. In plain language: grenade usage should now make more sense and match player expectations better.

## Medical gameplay now supports rescue, not just defeat

One of the biggest additions in this update is the new medical state. Players can now enter a **persistent unconscious state**, opening the door for rescue gameplay instead of immediate hard stops.

That rescue loop includes a **timed trauma-kit interaction**, which gives medics, teammates, or bystanders a more believable way to respond. This matters a lot for a future **Cyberpunk 2077 RP server**, because it turns “you went down” into an actual scene instead of a simple fail state.

The presentation got upgraded too. The death screen was redesigned with a darker fade, animated lifeline styling, and a more fitting cyberpunk visual identity. It is still a failure state, but it now looks and feels like part of Night City rather than a placeholder overlay.

## Vehicle customization moves closer to real ownership

Cars are part of player identity in Night City, so this update pushed vehicle customization forward in a big way. The Auto Studio now supports **paid paint jobs**, complete with **live previews** so players can see the result before spending their money.

An **orbit camera** was added to the customization view as well, making it much easier to inspect the full vehicle during paint selection. The workshop layout and controls were also refined, and the gameplay HUD is hidden in that experience to keep the presentation cleaner.

Most importantly, paint customization is now **persistent**. If a player styles a vehicle, that look is meant to stick. For a **Cyberpunk 2077 online** experience, that kind of persistence is a big part of making possessions feel real.

This update also enabled both Auto Studio access points, helping the system feel more complete as an in-world feature rather than a one-off test location.

## Safe zone tools improve server moderation

Server staff were not forgotten. Light RP now includes **English-language staff menus** for managing safe zones.

That is a practical improvement for moderation, event hosting, and rule enforcement. Safe zones are one of those systems that can quietly shape the tone of a server, and making them easier to control is useful for future **Cyberpunk 2077 dedicated server** operators who want cleaner admin workflows.

## A better default starting point for the female seed

Outside of Light RP, the base project updated the default female starter save. The previous version began in a prologue-locked state, which is exactly the part of Cyberpunk 2077 where story-specific roadblocks and world-state restrictions can cause awkward behavior.

The new default starts from a more suitable **vanilla Act 2** save instead. That should reduce strange world-state problems and create a better foundation for multiplayer sessions in Night City.

## Why this update matters

This was a strong gameplay-focused day for OPEN//77. Inventory usability, rescue systems, persistent customization, safer moderation tools, and a cleaner start state all push the project toward a more believable **Cyberpunk 2077 co-op** and RP experience.

None of this is flashy marketing-only work. These are the systems that shape how a session actually feels minute to minute: how fast you can equip gear, whether a medic can save you, whether your car keeps its style, and whether admins can manage the world without friction.

That is the kind of progress that turns a cool idea into a **Night City online** experience people can imagine living in.
