OPEN//77 Stable 143 is now live, and the headline feature is **environmental proximity voice**. This update pushes Cyberpunk 2077 multiplayer closer to the kind of lived-in social space players expect from Night City online: conversations feel local, encounters feel physical, and the world feels less like a menu with avatars.

## Environmental proximity voice changes the feel of the world

Voice is one of the biggest immersion multipliers in any multiplayer game. Text chat can carry information, but voice carries presence. With environmental proximity voice in Stable 143, OPEN//77 takes an important step toward making Cyberpunk 2077 online feel like a real place instead of a disconnected session.

The key idea is simple: communication now behaves more like it belongs inside the environment. When voice follows proximity rules, player interactions become easier to read and more believable. A conversation on a sidewalk feels different from a meetup in a club, a quick exchange during a street stop feels different from a long RP scene, and the distance between players starts to matter in a way that supports immersion instead of breaking it.

For a Cyberpunk 2077 co-op or RP experience, that matters a lot. Small social moments are often what make a multiplayer world memorable. A natural hello, overhearing a nearby discussion, gathering a crew before a job, or meeting strangers in a district all become stronger when the audio experience matches the space around the players.

## Stable 143 is now published

This update is not just in testing anymore. **Stable client 143** has been published, which means the latest player-facing build is officially moving forward on the OPEN//77 platform.

For players waiting for visible progress, this is the kind of release that stands out. Some updates improve background systems, while others directly change how the game feels the moment you log in. Proximity voice lands firmly in the second category. It is the kind of feature that can instantly make a Cyberpunk 2077 multiplayer mod feel more social, more readable, and more grounded.

For future communities and server projects, this also matters because voice is a foundational part of many plans for a Cyberpunk 2077 RP server. Roleplay, freeroam meetups, event hosting, faction scenes, and simple street-level interactions all benefit from voice behaving in a consistent world-based way.

## What this means for players right now

The immediate win is atmosphere. Night City should feel noisy, close, and alive. A multiplayer layer only works if player presence feels anchored in the same space, and voice is a huge part of that illusion.

Stable 143 should make ordinary moments more interesting:

- meeting other players in public spaces
- holding local conversations without everything feeling global
- creating cleaner RP scenes and social gatherings
- making the city feel more inhabited during exploration

This is also one of those features that helps both casual play and organized servers. Players looking for simple Cyberpunk 2077 co-op fun get better moment-to-moment interaction, while server owners building a larger Cyberpunk 2077 dedicated server experience get a more convincing social layer to build on.

## Creator support keeps pace with the live build

Alongside the stable release, there is also a fresh devkit snapshot aligned with the current server build. That update is mainly for creators and technical community members, helping resources and scripting work stay in sync with the version that is moving forward.

That is not the flashy part of today’s news, but it is still useful. As OPEN//77 grows, keeping creator-facing tooling aligned with live platform progress helps reduce friction for server development and future gameplay experiments.

## A small feature with a big multiplayer impact

Environmental proximity voice might sound like a single feature on paper, but in practice it improves one of the most important parts of any CP2077 multiplayer experience: how players feel each other’s presence.

That is why today’s update matters. Stable 143 is not just another number. It is a meaningful quality step for OPEN//77 as a Cyberpunk 2077 multiplayer mod, and it moves the project closer to a version of Night City that feels truly shared.

More world, more atmosphere, more believable social play — that is a good day’s work.
