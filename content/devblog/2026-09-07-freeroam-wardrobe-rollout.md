OPEN//77 moved a fuller gameplay package into place today: Freeroam activities, wardrobe support, admin tooling, and a freshly verified client release are now lined up together. For anyone following this Cyberpunk 2077 multiplayer mod, this is the kind of progress that makes the sandbox feel more complete rather than just more experimental.

## Freeroam is becoming a real shared sandbox

The headline change is the restored full Freeroam deployment. That package now brings the current activity work and wardrobe readiness together instead of treating them like separate experiments.

For players, that matters because a good **Cyberpunk 2077 online** experience is not just about connecting to a server. It also needs things to do once you arrive. Bringing Race and PvP activity work into the broader Freeroam setup is a step toward that goal. Instead of isolated feature branches, OPEN//77 is shaping a shared space where multiple systems can coexist.

That sounds simple, but integration is where multiplayer projects usually get tested. Features can work perfectly on their own and still clash once they are combined. Getting activities and wardrobe support back into one deployment is the important part of today’s work.

## Wardrobe support stayed in the rollout

Wardrobe readiness remained part of the merged deployment, which is good news for anyone who cares about identity and presentation in Night City. In a social sandbox, character look is not just cosmetic polish. It is part of how players recognize each other, build personas, and eventually support things like events or a **Cyberpunk 2077 RP server** style experience.

Keeping wardrobe support present during a broader Freeroam integration also shows the team is trying to avoid the usual trap of "gameplay now, customization later." OPEN//77 needs both to land together if it wants the world to feel social from day one.

## UI and HUD updates are getting leaner

Another meaningful part of the update is the work on the WebUI and HUD update flow. The project now includes optimizations aimed at handling interface updates more efficiently, with a GPU-first direction for the UI path.

For players, the short version is simple: menus and HUD elements need to react quickly without becoming a performance tax. In a **Cyberpunk 2077 co-op** or multiplayer environment, UI is constantly doing work. Health, prompts, activities, notifications, and interaction hints all need to stay in sync with the world. Any efficiency gains here are valuable because they help the game feel smoother while more systems are active at once.

The merge also kept earlier fixes related to interface visibility and HUD behavior, which helps protect progress already made in recent builds.

## Stability work matters even when it is not flashy

Today’s integration also preserved recovery checks and diagnostics from upstream work. That is not a headline-grabbing feature, but it is the kind of improvement that helps a multiplayer session survive edge cases instead of failing hard.

This matters even more in a **Cyberpunk 2077 dedicated server** environment, where reliability is part of the product. Server owners and testers need builds that are easier to validate, easier to deploy, and less likely to regress when multiple game systems are merged together.

There was also care taken to keep important content streaming behavior intact during the integration process, which reduces the chance of one major feature breaking unrelated parts of the game world.

## Verified client build published

On the platform side, OPEN//77 published a newly verified client build to the CDN. That is an important operational step because multiplayer testing works best when everyone is on the same known-good version.

A verified build does not automatically mean a public floodgate opening, but it does mean the rollout is becoming more structured. The team is not just building features in isolation; it is also preparing the delivery path that will eventually support broader testing for this **Cyberpunk 2077 multiplayer** experience.

## Why this day matters

Today was less about a single flashy reveal and more about combining several important pieces into one usable package. Freeroam activities, wardrobe support, admin tools, UI optimization, and a verified client build all push OPEN//77 toward a more believable version of Night City online.

That is the exciting part: not just more features, but more features living together without falling apart. For a CP2077 multiplayer project, that is real progress.
